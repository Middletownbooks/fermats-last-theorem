DESIGN AS CONFIGURED
  20 arms x 15 items x 2 reps = 600 main runs (1800 API calls)
  pilot: 15 items x 3 reps = 45 runs (135 calls)
  models: answer claude-opus-5, judges claude-opus-5 + claude-sonnet-5

PER-RUN TOKEN ESTIMATE (chars / 4.0; replace with usage after batch 1)
  answer in  ~   1475   answer out ~   4000 (incl. adaptive thinking)
  judge in   ~   4873 x2  judge out  ~    800 x2

COST
  pilot             45 runs   ~   0.5M in  ~  0.3M out   ~$8
  main study       600 runs   ~   6.7M in  ~  3.4M out   ~$102
  pilot + main     645 runs   ~   7.2M in  ~  3.6M out   ~$109

POWER: can the design reach its own threshold?
  runs per factor level: 300  (20.0 per problem per level)
  assumed ICC 0.1 -> design effect 2.90; assumed base rate 40%
  smallest detectable difference at 80% power: 19.4 points

  The pre-registered target is ~30 points. The configured design REACHES it.
  to detect 30 points:    122 runs per level ->   300 main runs  ~$51
  to detect 20 points:    281 runs per level ->   600 main runs  ~$102
  to detect 15 points:    501 runs per level ->  1200 main runs  ~$203
  to detect 10 points:   1123 runs per level ->  2400 main runs  ~$407

  THE BINDING CONSTRAINT IS THE DESIGN EFFECT, NOT THE BUDGET.
  Clustering by problem inflates the variance by the factor above, and it is driven by
  the SMALL NUMBER OF PROBLEMS, not the number of runs. Adding reps on 15 problems buys
  progressively less; adding problems buys more per dollar. If the pilot leaves fewer
  than ~12 usable problems, no affordable number of reps reaches 30 points, and the
  right response is to enlarge the battery rather than to buy more runs.

  Also unbudgeted: continuations when a reply is cut off (up to 1 per run), and re-runs after any failed batch.
