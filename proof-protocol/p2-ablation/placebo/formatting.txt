Number every displayed equation consecutively at the right margin, in parentheses, and refer to equations only by those numbers. Do not restart numbering inside sections.

Use sentence case for all headings. Headings carry no terminal punctuation. Leave one blank line before a heading and none after it.

Write numerals below ten as words in running prose unless they are mathematical values. Use a thin space between a number and its unit, and never begin a sentence with a symbol.

Cite sources in author-year form. When two works share an author and year, distinguish them with lower-case letters in order of first appearance. Do not use ibid. or op. cit.

Set variables in italic and operators such as sin, log and det in roman. Prefer solidus fractions in running text and built-up fractions only in displays.

Keep lines under one hundred characters. Break a long display before a relation sign, and align continuation lines on that sign. Do not break inside a pair of delimiters.

Use the serial comma. Use an en dash for ranges and between the names of joint discoverers, a hyphen for compound modifiers, and no spaces around either.

Abbreviations are defined at first use and not again. Do not pluralise symbols with an apostrophe. Latin abbreviations are followed by a comma.

Lists of three or fewer items are run into the sentence. Longer lists are displayed, each item beginning lower-case and ending with a semicolon, the last with a full stop.

Footnotes are not used. Parenthetical remarks longer than one line are promoted to their own sentence.

Theorem environments are numbered in a single sequence with the displayed equations, so that Theorem 4 may be followed by equation (5). Do not maintain separate counters.

A proof ends with an open square at the right margin. Where a proof ends with a display, place the square on its own line, flush right, rather than beside the display.

Reserve boldface for vectors and for the first occurrence of a defined term. Do not use it for emphasis in running prose; recast the sentence instead.

Quantifiers in displayed formulas are written as symbols; in running prose they are written as words. Do not mix the two conventions within a single sentence.

Set-builder notation uses a vertical bar with thin spaces on both sides, never a colon, and the bar is sized to the taller of the two expressions it separates.

Matrices are delimited by square brackets, determinants by vertical bars. Do not use parentheses for either. Block matrices carry thin rules between blocks.

Where a symbol is defined by an equation rather than derived, mark the equality with a small triangle above the equals sign at that one occurrence only.

Subscripts that are words rather than indices are set in roman: write the maximum with a roman subscript and the i-th entry with an italic one.

The argument of a function is enclosed in parentheses unless the function is a single letter applied to a single letter, in which case the parentheses may be dropped.

Ellipses between commas sit on the baseline; ellipses between operators sit at the vertical centre of the line. Never use three full stops typed by hand.

Punctuation following a display belongs to the sentence, not the display: a comma or full stop is set immediately after the closing delimiter with no preceding space.

Cross-references name the object and its number together, capitalised: write Theorem 3 and Section 2, not theorem 3 or the previous section.

Where a constant is absolute, say so at its first appearance and thereafter write it without qualification. Where it depends on a parameter, show that dependence in the notation.

Tables carry their captions above; figures carry their captions below. Both are numbered in their own sequences and both are referred to by number, never by position.

Column headings in tables are set in sentence case without terminal punctuation. Numeric columns align on the decimal separator; text columns align left.

An appendix is lettered rather than numbered, and its equations carry the letter as a prefix, so that the first display of Appendix A is (A.1).

Acknowledgements precede the bibliography and are set as an unnumbered section. Funding statements belong there, not in a footnote to the title.

The bibliography is ordered alphabetically by first author's surname, then by year, then by title. Do not order it by first citation.

Preprint references carry the archive identifier and the year of first posting, not the year of the version consulted. Published versions supersede preprint entries entirely.

Page ranges in references are given in full: write the closing page number without eliding its leading digits.

Journal names are abbreviated according to the standard list; where a journal is absent from that list, its name is given in full.

Titles of cited works retain the capitalisation of their originals, except that an initial capital is supplied where the original has none.

Where a result is attributed to unpublished work, name the form of the communication and its year in the sentence, and omit it from the bibliography.

Author names in the bibliography are given as surname followed by initials, with no space between initials and a comma before the final conjunction.

The abstract is a single paragraph of at most two hundred words. It contains no citations, no displayed formulas and no defined symbols.

Keywords follow the abstract, separated by semicolons, in lower case except for proper nouns, and are not terminated by a full stop.

The title carries no terminal punctuation and no abbreviations. A subtitle, if any, is separated by a colon and begins with a capital.

Running heads carry the author surnames on the verso and a shortened title on the recto, each in small capitals and each under fifty characters.

Section numbering is decimal to at most three levels. A fourth level is set as a run-in heading in italic, terminated by a full stop, with no number.

Displayed quotations are set in a smaller size, indented on the left only, with no quotation marks, and are followed by the attribution in parentheses.

Where a proof is deferred, say where it appears at the point of deferral, and repeat the statement in full at that later point rather than referring back.

Hypotheses are labelled with capital letters in parentheses at the point of statement, and are referred to by those labels wherever they are assumed.

A remark that qualifies a theorem immediately follows it, before the proof. A remark that extends it follows the proof. Do not interleave the two kinds.

Notation introduced for a single proof is confined to that proof and is not used again. Notation used throughout is collected in a table at the end of the introduction.

Where two notations for the same object are current in the literature, choose one, say in a sentence which one, and do not alternate between them.

Indices run over explicit ranges stated at their first use. An index whose range is clear from context is still given a range at least once.

Summation limits are placed above and below the sign in displays, and beside it in running prose. Empty sums are declared to be zero at their first occurrence.

The empty product is declared to be one, and the empty maximum is declared to be negative infinity, each at first use, and neither is left to inference.

Inequalities that hold for all sufficiently large values of a parameter say so explicitly. Do not leave the threshold implicit in an asymptotic symbol.

Asymptotic notation is defined in the introduction, including whether the implied constants may depend on fixed parameters and which parameters those are.

Where a bound is stated with an unspecified constant, the constant is named, and its dependence on the problem's data is stated in the same sentence.

Numerical values are given to the precision that the computation supports, and the precision is stated. Do not report digits the method cannot justify.

Computations carried out by machine name the software and its version in the text, and the code is described in an appendix or deposited with the article.

Algorithms are displayed in a numbered environment with the input and output stated on separate lines before the first step, each labelled in small capitals.

Steps within an algorithm are numbered, and loops are indicated by indentation rather than by explicit end markers. Comments are set in roman after a double slash.

Complexity claims state the model of computation and the cost measure. A bound on the number of operations is distinguished from a bound on the running time.

Where a figure is generated from data, the data's source is named in the caption and the plotting conventions are stated there rather than in the text.

Axes on plots are labelled with the quantity and its unit, separated by a solidus. Logarithmic axes state their base. Legends sit inside the plot area.

Colour is never the only carrier of information in a figure: distinguish series additionally by line style or by marker shape.

The same quantity keeps the same symbol throughout the article, including in figures and tables, and including where a source used a different one.

Where an article corrects an earlier one, the correction is stated in the introduction with a precise reference, not merely implied by the new result.

Where a definition is standard but contested, say whose definition you are using, and give the reference in the sentence that introduces it.

Equations that are referred to only once may be set inline if they fit; equations referred to more than once are displayed and numbered regardless of length.

The introduction states the main result in full before the notation section, even at the cost of forward references, which are given explicitly.

Where a proof occupies more than two pages, it opens with a paragraph naming its steps in order, and each step is announced by a run-in heading.

A statement proved only under an additional assumption carries that assumption in its statement, not in a remark following it.

Symbols that differ only by typeface are not used for different objects in the same article, however clear the distinction appears to the author.
